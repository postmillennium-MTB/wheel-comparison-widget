EI_lat (N·m²)
Rim lateral bending stiffness. Resists the rim bowing sideways. Typically 40–80 N·m² for alloy double-wall rims. Higher = stiffer rim shell, but GJ usually dominates.
EI_rad (N·m²)
Rim radial (in-plane) bending stiffness. Governs how the rim distributes load around its hoop. Typically 3–10× higher than EI_lat for most rims (100–300 N·m²).
GJ (N·m²)
Rim torsional stiffness. Because the rim is curved, bending and twisting are coupled — GJ dominates lateral flexibility more than EI_lat does (Ford §3.4). Typical alloy rims: 15–35 N·m².
EA_rim (MN)
Rim axial (hoop) stiffness. Used to compute the compressive hoop load that spoke tension induces in the rim, which softens lateral stiffness. Typical value 8–15 MN for alloy rims.
Modes N
Number of Fourier modes included in the Mode Matrix sum. Each mode captures a different deformation shape of the rim. Results converge by ~16–24; higher values add precision at negligible cost.
